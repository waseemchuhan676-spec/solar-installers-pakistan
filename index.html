Net metering has changed — read before you sign

Under NEPRA’s Prosumer Regulations 2026, net billing replaced net metering for new applicants: exported units are credited at roughly Rs 11/unit instead of ~Rs 26/unit before, and contracts run 5 years instead of 7 (per ProChuno’s summary of Tribune / Arab News reporting, Feb 2026). An installer promising you the old net-metering rates is quoting terms that no longer apply to new connections — ask them to show you the current DISCO rules for your city.

Before you sign anything

Four checks that come straight out of how this directory’s data was verified — they cost nothing and filter out most bad installs.

Photo: PeopleLake Asia (peoplelake.asia).

  1. 1Verify the certificate yourself. Certification below is shown exactly as the sources state it — some entries come from the PPIB list of Oct 2024 whose validity dates have passed, and some from a transcription of the 29 Oct 2026 list. A lapsed date doesn’t prove a company lost certification (firms renew), but always re-check the current list at ppib.gov.pk before paying an advance.
  2. 2Get the quote in writing, with brands named. Only 3 of these 43 companies publish prices. Everyone else quotes after a survey — insist the written quote names the panel brand and wattage, inverter brand, battery chemistry and structure type, so you can compare like with like.
  3. 3Ask who files the net-billing paperwork. Under the 2026 prosumer rules the export rate and contract length changed. Companies marked “Net metering” above handle DISCO paperwork — confirm they will file under the new net-billing terms for your city (K-Electric, LESCO, IESCO, MEPCO, PESCO, QESCO).
  4. 4Call the number on the company’s own site. Every phone here was copied from the company’s own page or an official list on 9 Nov 2026. If a number has changed, the company’s website (linked on each card) is the safest place to get the new one — not a random blog list.

An honest note on coverage

Quetta has only 3 listings because only 3 Quetta installers could be verified against their own page or an official list — the list was not padded with unverified names. LocalePak’s entire solar category for Pakistan holds just 9 listings, which is why most entries here come from company websites and the PPIB/AEDB lists instead. Companies with offices in several cities are listed once, under their head-office city, with their other offices noted on the card. “Not published” on a card means the field was searched for and not found in a public source — it was never guessed.
